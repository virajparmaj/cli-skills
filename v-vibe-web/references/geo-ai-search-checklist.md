# AI-search discovery review

Review how public, intended content can be found and understood. Apply the entrypoint’s evidence, scoring, and deferral policy. Citation and recommendation behavior varies by product and query; do not claim knowledge of a proprietary ranking formula or promise inclusion.

## Content and evidence

- Check that the product name, purpose, audience, and core behavior are described clearly and truthfully. Question headings and direct answers can help readers where natural; exact wording and token counts are not universal retrieval requirements.
- Prefer original, supportable facts and useful documentation. Do not fabricate benchmark data, testimonials, certifications, author credentials, or freshness dates.
- Use structured data only when it matches visible content and applicable current documentation. Accurate metadata is useful; it does not guarantee a citation or rich result.
- Comparison or alternatives pages are optional product/content work. Add them only when requested or part of the user’s actual content plan.

## Crawler access and rendering

- Identify the specific search product and its documented crawler before judging access. Search crawlers, user-triggered fetchers, and training crawlers may have different purposes and controls. Preserve the user’s deliberate training/search policy; do not unblock every AI bot to chase visibility.
- Inspect relevant `robots.txt` groups, live headers, public route access, and rendered content. Do not treat a string match as the effective crawler rule.
- Test whether critical explanations require interaction or unavailable JavaScript. Server/pre-rendering is an option when needed, not an automatic framework migration.
- Mark account, indexing, and citation behavior unavailable to this audit as **UNVERIFIED**. Check current official crawler documentation before proposing rule changes.

## Observation and follow-up

If the user asks for discovery testing, record product/model, date, exact prompts, returned sources, and observed inaccuracies. One prompt response is an observation, not a stable ranking metric. Recurring checks, outreach, public posts, submissions, and content expansion require their own requested scope and authorization.
